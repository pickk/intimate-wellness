Most wellness products don't fail because of manufacturing defects — they fail because of inconsistent aftercare. The good news is that correct maintenance is fast, cheap, and largely independent of brand. This guide covers the protocol that protects both your health and your investment, with material-specific notes for the categories you're most likely to own.

## Why aftercare is a health decision

Anything that contacts the body accumulates residue, moisture, and microorganisms with use. Non-porous materials can be fully sanitized; porous materials can only be surface-cleaned. In either case, skipping the post-use clean lets biofilm develop, which then becomes both a hygiene risk and the reason a product starts to smell, discolor, or feel tacky long before its time.

Aftercare has two goals: remove what the product picked up, and store it in a state that doesn't invite new contamination.

## The standard aftercare protocol

For most products, the post-use routine is the same four steps. Material-specific differences come in storage and sanitization depth.

```text
Standard post-use routine:
1. Rinse with warm water (never hot) to remove residue
2. Wash with unscented mild soap or a dedicated toy cleaner
3. Rinse fully — soap residue irritates sensitive tissue
4. Air-dry completely before storage (no damp surfaces)
```

Skipping step four is the most common mistake. Storing a slightly damp product in an enclosed space is how mildew begins, and it's nearly impossible to reverse once it starts.

## Material-specific maintenance

### Silicone

Warm water and mild soap is sufficient after normal use. For periodic deep sanitization, medical-grade silicone tolerates brief boiling (3–5 minutes) on most products — check the manufacturer's guidance first, since embedded electronics don't survive this. Store in a breathable pouch away from other silicone items; prolonged silicone-on-silicone contact causes surface degradation. Always pair with water-based lubricant — silicone lubes dissolve silicone surfaces over time.

### ABS plastic

The simplest material to maintain. Wash with soap and warm water, dry, and store. ABS is chemically inert to most lubricants, so compatibility is rarely an issue. The main risk is surface micro-scratches from abrasive storage containers — keep it in a soft pouch.

### TPE and porous materials

These require the most diligent aftercare and still carry residual risk. Wash thoroughly, dry fully, and apply a renewing powder (cornstarch-based, not talc) to prevent the surface from becoming tacky. Store individually in a cool, dry place away from other products — porous materials can leach and react with neighbors. Plan to replace these sooner than non-porous alternatives.

## Storage principles

Storage is where lifespan is won or lost. A clean product stored badly still degrades.

- **Cool, dry, dark.** Heat and UV light break down most materials over time.
- **Breathable containers.** Fabric pouches allow residual moisture to escape; sealed plastic bags trap it.
- **Separation.** Don't let products touch each other in storage, especially silicone-to-silicone.
- **Away from direct sunlight.** UV is a silent killer of color and flexibility.

## Knowing when to retire a product

No maintenance protocol runs forever. Replace a product when any of these appear:

- Visible cracks, tears, or pitting on the surface
- Persistent odor that survives a full clean
- Tackiness or a sticky residue that won't wash off
- Discoloration that signals material breakdown
- Any electronic function becoming intermittent

A reasonable rule of thumb: non-porous products with consistent care last years; porous products last months. Treat the replacement schedule as a health decision, not a cost decision — the math favors retiring early over using something whose material integrity you can't verify.
