Hygiene is the least glamorous part of wellness, and the part most people get wrong. The good news: correct practice is simple, fast, and dramatically extends the life of your products while protecting your health. This guide covers cleaning, storage, and knowing when to retire a product.

## Why material drives the protocol

Cleaning rules follow directly from material porosity, which is why knowing what your product is made of matters. Non-porous materials (silicone, ABS plastic) can be fully sanitized. Porous materials (TPE, jelly-like blends) can never be fully sterilized and require more diligent cleaning and storage.

If you skipped the buying guide, the short version: buy non-porous whenever possible.

## The basic cleaning protocol

After every use — no exceptions — follow this sequence:

1. Rinse with warm water (not hot).
2. Wash with mild, unscented soap or a dedicated cleaner.
3. Rinse thoroughly to remove all residue.
4. Pat dry with a lint-free cloth, then air-dry fully.
5. Store only once completely dry.

```bash
# Daily routine — keep it under two minutes
warm_rinse
soap_wash          # mild, unscented
rinse_completely
pat_dry            # lint-free cloth
air_dry            # full dry before storage
```

Skipping the dry step is the most common mistake. Storing a damp product in a closed container invites microbial growth that no amount of soap will fix later.

## Deep cleaning and sanitizing

For non-porous materials, periodic sanitizing is straightforward. Boilable silicone (check the manufacturer's note first — not all silicone products are boil-safe) can be submerged for three minutes. Otherwise, use a 10% bleach solution, rinse thoroughly, and dry completely. Never boil TPE or any porous material; heat degrades it.

## Storage that prevents damage

Storage is where most products degrade prematurely. Follow these basics:

- Store in a cool, dry, dark place — away from direct sunlight and heat sources.
- Keep silicone products from touching other silicone items; prolonged contact can cause surface degradation.
- Use breathable pouches rather than sealed plastic bags.
- Remove batteries from battery-operated devices during long-term storage.

## Lubricant compatibility

Lubricant choice affects both comfort and product longevity. The rule most people miss: **silicone-based lubricants degrade silicone products.** Pair silicone products with water-based lubricants. For non-silicone materials, check the manufacturer's guidance.

| Product material | Recommended lube | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silicone | Water-based | Silicone-based |
| ABS plastic | Water- or silicone-based | Oil-based (staining) |
| TPE | Water-based only | Silicone- and oil-based |

## When to replace a product

Even well-maintained products have a lifespan. Replace immediately if you notice:

- Visible tears, cracks, or pitting on the surface.
- A persistent odor that doesn't resolve with cleaning.
- Texture changes — stickiness, hardening, or tackiness.
- Discoloration that won't wash away.

Porous materials typically need replacing sooner than non-porous ones. As a rough guideline, inspect every three months and trust what you see and smell over any fixed schedule.

## The two-minute habit

None of this requires elaborate effort. The difference between someone whose products last years and someone replacing them annually is almost always the same: a consistent two-minute cleaning and drying routine. Build the habit once and it runs on autopilot — and your body, and your wallet, will thank you.
